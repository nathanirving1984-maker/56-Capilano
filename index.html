01 — The property

Capilano Drive climbs the ridge behind Marin Country Club and stops. What sits at the top of it has been looking out over three separate pieces of Marin since 1963.

Sixty-four hundred square feet is a number that only means something once you understand what it is doing here. This is a house built wide rather than tall, laid along the crown of a 1.13-acre parcel so that its rooms face outward — seven bedrooms and nine baths arranged to give a large household, or several smaller ones, the rare combination of proximity and distance.

The parcel does the work that no floor plan can. It is a hilltop, which in this part of Novato means the neighbors are below you and the view is not a borrowed one. Water to the east, valley to the south, fairways to the west. The house was sited to take all three, and six decades later that decision is still the most valuable thing about it.

02 — Three horizons

Most hilltops give you one view. This one gives three, and they are not variations on each other — they are water, valley, and fairway, in three different directions, at three different distances.

I

San Pablo Bay

East · open water · long view

The distance view, and the one that changes most. San Pablo Bay sits east beyond the low ground, flat and bright in the afternoon and doing something different with the light every hour of the day. It is the horizon that makes the house feel like it is somewhere specific rather than simply up high.

II

Ignacio Valley

South · valley floor · ridgelines

The middle distance. The valley runs south below the ridge, held on both sides by hills that stay green into early summer and go gold for the rest of it. This is the view that gives the property its sense of enclosure — open, but framed, with the protected open space of Ignacio and Indian Valley beyond the far edge.

III

Marin Country Club

West · fairways · immediate foreground

The near view, and the reason this street exists. The club's fairways run directly below the ridge to the west — mown, watered, and permanently unbuildable, which makes them the most reliable foreground a house can have. Capilano Drive was laid out to sit above them.

03 — The residence

Seven bedrooms

Room to separate

Seven is past the point where a house is simply large and into the range where it can hold guests, staff, extended family, or a working household without anyone being on top of anyone else.

Nine bathrooms

A bath for every bedroom, and two spare

Nine baths against seven bedrooms is the detail that tells you how the house was planned. Private quarters stay private; the public rooms are served independently.

6,469 square feet

Built wide, not tall

The interior is laid along the crown of the ridge rather than stacked against it — which is why so much of the square footage gets an exterior wall, and with it, a horizon.

1.13 acres

The whole hilltop

An acre and an eighth at the top of the ridge, which buys the two things that cannot be renovated in later: the setback from the road and the absence of anything above you.

Built 1963

Original bones

A 1963 build on a view parcel of this size means generous ceiling runs and rooms sized to their era — structure worth keeping, and a canvas for whatever comes next.

Orientation

Sited for the light

East for the morning off the bay, west for the long end of the day over the fairways. The siting is the design decision the house has been living off for sixty years.
